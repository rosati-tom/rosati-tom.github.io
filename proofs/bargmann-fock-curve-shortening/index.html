\( \newcommand{\R}{\mathbb R} \newcommand{\Z}{\mathbb Z} \newcommand{\E}{\mathbb E} \newcommand{\PP}{\mathbb P} \newcommand{\HH}{\mathcal H} \newcommand{\dd}{\,\mathrm d} \newcommand{\ind}{\mathbf 1} \newcommand{\diam}{\operatorname{diam}} \newcommand{\dist}{\operatorname{dist}} \newcommand{\supp}{\operatorname{supp}} \newcommand{\vol}{\operatorname{vol}} \newcommand{\cl}{\operatorname{cl}} \newcommand{\Tr}{\operatorname{Tr}} \newcommand{\cross}{\operatorname{Cross}} \newcommand{\eps}{\varepsilon} \)

Abstract. We give a proposed proof of an upper bound, within a logarithmic factor of the diffusive rate, for the expected length density of planar Bargmann–Fock nodal curves evolved by curve shortening. The argument has two parts: a local length–curvature inequality combined with monotonicity of crossings between shrinking circles, and a bound on the expected number of initial nodal crossings using finite-range approximation, planar separation, and the product-space BKR inequality. The proof uses no exact percolation exponent. This is a research draft of an argument developed here, not an attribution of the conclusion to an existing publication. Classical inputs are explicitly identified.

1. Statement, normalization, and conventions

Let \(f\) be the centered stationary planar Bargmann–Fock field normalized by \[\E[f(x)f(y)]=\exp(-|x-y|^2/2). \tag{1.1}\] Almost surely its zero set is a locally finite union of disjoint smooth embedded compact curves. Evolve every such curve by \[\partial_t X=\partial_s^2X=\kappa n , \tag{1.2}\] where \(s\) denotes arclength at the current time. Delete a curve at its extinction time. Write \(\Gamma_t\) for the union of the remaining curves, \(\mu_t=\HH^1\!\restriction\Gamma_t\), and \[ Q=[0,1)^2,\qquad a(t)=\E\mu_t(Q). \] Thus the inverse-density coarsening scale is \(L(t)=1/a(t)\). All constants below may depend on the fixed normalization (1.1), and constants for a rectangle or annulus may depend on its fixed shape.

Theorem 1.1 (Claim proved in this draft). There are constants \(c,C>0\) and \(t_0>e^e\) such that \[\frac{c}{\sqrt t}\le a(t) \le \frac{C\log t}{\sqrt{t\,\log\log t}}, \qquad t\ge t_0. \tag{1.3}\] Consequently \[ c\,\frac{\sqrt{t\,\log\log t}}{\log t} \le L(t)\le C\sqrt t \] after changing the constants.

The two main steps of the proof are the local reduction in Proposition 3.3 and the crossing-multiplicity estimate in Theorem 4.1. The latter is deduced from published ingredients; it is not assumed as an additional percolation hypothesis. The distinction between the asserted logarithmic bound and the still unproved sharp bound is discussed in Section 6.

We use the following classical facts. The regularity and boundedness of the nodal components follow from Bulinskaya nondegeneracy and the absence of unbounded critical components in [3, Corollary 1.5]; see also [7, Lemma 5.8 and Corollary 5.7] for the loopwise interpretation of the planar flow. A smooth embedded plane curve has a unique classical curve-shortening flow until it shrinks to a point; embeddedness, avoidance, and convex-hull containment are preserved [5, 6]. For its length \(\ell_\gamma\), filled enclosed area \(A_\gamma\), and extinction time \(T_\gamma\), \[\ell_\gamma'(t)=-\int_{\gamma_t}\kappa^2\dd s,\qquad A_\gamma(t)=A_\gamma(0)-2\pi t,\qquad T_\gamma=\frac{A_\gamma(0)}{2\pi}, \tag{1.4}\] and \(\ell_\gamma(t)\to0\) at extinction. The filled area here includes any regions enclosed by other nested loops.

2. The stationary flow and its energy identity

Lemma 2.1 (Local finiteness). Almost surely, for every compact spatial set and every finite time interval, only finitely many initial nodal components have descendants meeting that set during the interval.

Proof. A regular zero set has only finitely many global connected components meeting a given compact set: cover its intersection with the compact set by finitely many implicit-function neighborhoods, each with a connected local zero set. Every component meeting the compact set contains one of these local pieces.

Fix \(r,T<\infty\), and choose \(R>\sqrt{r^2+2T}\). If an initial loop does not meet the closed disk \(\overline B_R\), it lies outside the circle \(\partial B_R\). Avoidance with the shrinking circle of radius \(\sqrt{R^2-2u}\), \(0\le u\le T\), keeps that loop outside this circle for as long as it exists. This circle still contains \(B_r\) throughout the interval. Thus only the finitely many initial loops meeting \(\overline B_R\) can have descendants entering \(B_r\) before \(T\). ∎

The construction is independent of the enumeration of the initial loops. Uniqueness and Euclidean covariance of classical curve shortening imply that its law is stationary and isotropic. The random measures used below are measurable: one may enumerate the initial components using countably many local charts, use continuous dependence of the classical flow on smooth initial curves before extinction, and then use Lemma 2.1. Extinction is handled by setting the component measure equal to zero. No translation-equivariant choice of a distinguished point on a loop is needed.

Lemma 2.2 (Stationary mass transport). If a nonnegative random measure \(M\) on \(\R^2\times\R^2\) is stationary under simultaneous translations of its two coordinates, then \[\E M(Q\times\R^2)=\E M(\R^2\times Q), \tag{2.1}\] with equality also allowed to take the value \(+\infty\).

Proof. Partition \(\R^2\) into the half-open squares \(Q+z\), \(z\in\Z^2\). Stationarity and Tonelli's theorem give \[ \E M(\R^2\times Q) =\sum_{z\in\Z^2}\E M((Q+z)\times Q) =\sum_{z\in\Z^2}\E M(Q\times(Q-z)) =\E M(Q\times\R^2). \] ∎

Let \(\mu_{\gamma,t}\) denote the arclength measure on a single descendant and put \(\ell_\gamma(t)=\mu_{\gamma,t}(\R^2)\), with both zero after extinction. Define \[M_t(\dd x,\dd y)= \sum_\gamma \frac{\mu_{\gamma,0}(\dd x)\mu_{\gamma,t}(\dd y)} {\ell_\gamma(0)} . \tag{2.2}\] Its incoming marginal is \(\mu_t\), whereas its outgoing marginal is \(\sum_\gamma(\ell_\gamma(t)/\ell_\gamma(0))\mu_{\gamma,0}\). Since length decreases, (2.1) yields \[a(t)=\E\sum_\gamma \frac{\ell_\gamma(t)}{\ell_\gamma(0)}\mu_{\gamma,0}(Q) \le a(0)=\frac12 . \tag{2.3}\] The last value follows directly from the one-point Kac–Rice formula: \(f(x)\) is independent of \(\nabla f(x)\), the latter has law \(N(0,I_2)\), and \((2\pi)^{-1/2}\E|N(0,I_2)|=1/2\).

Define the curvature measure and its intensity by \[ \nu_t(B)=\int_{\Gamma_t\cap B}\kappa^2\dd s,\qquad D(t)=\E\nu_t(Q). \]

Lemma 2.3 (Integrated dissipation). For \(0\le u<v<\infty\), \[a(u)-a(v)=\int_u^v D(w)\dd w. \tag{2.4}\] In particular \(a\) is locally absolutely continuous, \(D(t)<\infty\) for almost every \(t\), and \(a'(t)=-D(t)\) almost everywhere.

Proof. For each initial loop the first identity in (1.4), including extinction by continuity of length, gives \[ \ell_\gamma(u)-\ell_\gamma(v) =\int_u^v \nu_{\gamma,w}(\R^2)\dd w . \] Subtract the two finite expressions in (2.3). All resulting terms are nonnegative, so Tonelli applies. For each \(w\), apply Lemma 2.2 to \[ \sum_\gamma \frac{\mu_{\gamma,0}(\dd x)\nu_{\gamma,w}(\dd y)} {\ell_\gamma(0)} . \] Its incoming intensity is \(D(w)\); its outgoing intensity is exactly the summand obtained after Tonelli. This proves (2.4), without differentiating a spatially localized flow integral. ∎

For every bounded Borel set \(B\), stationarity therefore gives \[\E\mu_t(B)=a(t)|B|,\qquad \E\nu_t(B)=D(t)|B|, \tag{2.5}\] where the second identity is valid as an extended nonnegative identity and will be used at the almost-everywhere times for which \(D(t)<\infty\).

3. A local reduction using two shrinking circles

For \(0<r_1<r_2\), a through-arc of a smooth curve in the annulus \(B_{r_2}\setminus\overline B_{r_1}\) is the closure of a connected component of its intersection with the open annulus whose two endpoints belong to different boundary circles. When both circles are transverse to the curve, these are compact proper arcs. Let \(N_t(r_1,r_2)\) count all such arcs of \(\Gamma_t\). At transverse radii, different through-arcs are disjoint even at their endpoints: at a transverse boundary intersection only one of the two adjacent curve segments lies in the annulus.

Lemma 3.1 (Shrinking-annulus monotonicity). Fix \(t>0\) and \(r>0\). For almost every realization, \[N_t(r,2r)\le N_0\!\left(\sqrt{r^2+2t},\sqrt{4r^2+2t}\right). \tag{3.1}\]

Proof. First work with one deterministic evolving loop and transverse initial and terminal circles. For \(0\le u\le t\) use comparison radii \[ r_I(u)=\sqrt{r^2+2(t-u)},\qquad r_O(u)=\sqrt{4r^2+2(t-u)} . \] In a normal material parametrization define \(F(\theta,u)=|X(\theta,u)|^2+2u\). Since \(|\partial_sX|=1\), \[ \partial_u F=2X\cdot\partial_s^2X+2 =\partial_s^2F . \] Thus the two comparison-circle intersections are the zeros of two scalar parabolic solutions \(F-b_I\) and \(F-b_O\), with fixed levels \[ b_I=r^2+2t,\qquad b_O=4r^2+2t. \] On every compact pre-extinction interval the equation is uniformly parabolic on the parameter circle.

At a regular time record the intersection labels \(I,O\) in cyclic parameter order. Adjacent unequal labels correspond exactly to through-arcs. The local zero-number theorem [2] implies that at a multiple zero an even number of same-level intersections disappears, possibly leaving one intersection at an odd-order zero. Locally all these labels are identical, because \(b_I\ne b_O\). Intersections with different labels cannot collide or exchange their cyclic order. Hence the word changes only by deletion of adjacent equal-label pairs. Such a deletion cannot increase its number of unequal adjacent pairs: for neighboring labels \(a,b\) and the deleted label \(x\), \[ \ind_{\{a\ne b\}} \le \ind_{\{a\ne x\}}+\ind_{\{x\ne b\}} . \] This proves monotonicity for the loop. The local statement can also be obtained by applying the zero-number theorem on a small parameter interval with nonzero boundary values around each multiple zero. An identically zero level solution would make the loop coincide with a comparison circle; forward uniqueness rules this out when the terminal circles are transverse. A loop extinct before \(t\) contributes zero at the terminal time.

Summing the per-loop inequalities proves (3.1). The initial count is finite: every through-arc has length at least the positive width of the compact initial annulus, whose total nodal length is finite. Alternatively Lemma 2.1 reduces the sum to finitely many relevant loops.

It remains to justify transversality at the chosen deterministic initial and terminal radii. For any fixed smooth curve, centers of circles of a fixed radius tangent to it lie in the images of the two normal-offset maps \(x\mapsto x\pm r n(x)\), a planar null set. The same is true for a countable collection of curves. Stationarity and Tonelli's theorem imply that the probability that a circle about a fixed deterministic center is tangent to \(\Gamma_u\) is zero, for each fixed \(u,r\). This applies to the two endpoint times; intermediate tangencies were already handled by the zero-number theorem. ∎

Lemma 3.2 (Local length–curvature inequality). At transverse radii, for any locally finite union of smooth embedded loops, \[\mu(B_r)\le 4rN(r,2r)+4r^2\int_{\Gamma\cap B_{2r}}\kappa^2\dd s . \tag{3.2}\]

Proof. Consider a proper component \(\sigma\) of \(\Gamma\cap B_{2r}\) that meets \(B_r\). Its two endpoints are on \(\partial B_{2r}\). Writing \(T=\partial_sX\), \(\ell=\HH^1(\sigma)\), and \(E_\sigma=\int_\sigma\kappa^2\dd s\), integration by parts gives \[ \ell=[X\cdot T]_{\partial\sigma} -\int_\sigma X\cdot\kappa n\dd s \le4r+2r\sqrt{\ell E_\sigma} \le4r+\frac{\ell}{2}+2r^2E_\sigma . \] Consequently \(\ell\le8r+4r^2E_\sigma\). Each such component contains at least two distinct through-arcs, one entering the smaller disk and one leaving it. If a whole loop is contained in \(B_{2r}\), the boundary term vanishes; Cauchy–Schwarz directly gives \(\ell\le4r^2E_\sigma\). Sum these estimates over the disjoint components that meet \(B_r\). The number of proper components is at most \(N(r,2r)/2\), and their curvature energies sum to at most the energy in \(B_{2r}\). ∎

Proposition 3.3 (Stationary reduction). For every fixed \(r>0\) and almost every \(t>0\), \[a(t)\le \frac{4}{\pi r} \E N_0\!\left(\sqrt{r^2+2t},\sqrt{4r^2+2t}\right) +16r^2D(t). \tag{3.3}\] The same inequality holds for a deterministic choice \(r=r(t)>0\) for almost every \(t\).

Proof. Apply Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2, take expectations, and use (2.5). Dividing by \(\pi r^2\) gives the curvature coefficient \(4r^2|B_{2r}|/|B_r|=16r^2\). For a deterministic \(r(t)\), endpoint transversality holds with probability one for each fixed \(t\); Tonelli supplies the almost-everywhere statement in \(t\). The exceptional set where \(D(t)=\infty\) is null by Lemma 2.3. ∎

4. Initial crossing multiplicity

Fix \(a,b>0\), let \(D_R=[0,aR]\times[0,bR]\), and distinguish its left and right sides. A proper crosscut is an injective compact arc with one endpoint on each marked side and all its other points in the interior. Let \(N_R(g)\) be the maximum number of pairwise disjoint proper crosscuts of the zero set of \(g\) in \(D_R\). For regular zero sets transverse to the boundary and nonzero at corners, the crosscuts counted here are exactly the connected components of the zero set in the rectangle with endpoints on the marked sides. In particular \(N_R(f)\) is finite and bounded by the zero count on the left side. For measurability, restrict this definition to the open set of \(C^2\) fields with no zero-valued stratified critical point, and set the count equal to zero on its complement. On that open set the count is locally constant by the box-isotopy lemma used below; hence this extension is Borel measurable. The exceptional set has BF probability zero, and all applications to \(f_\rho\) below occur on the regular coupling event.

Theorem 4.1 (Crossing multiplicity). For the field (1.1), there are \(C,R_0>0\), depending only on \(a,b\), such that \[\E N_R(f)\le C\frac{\log R}{\sqrt{\log\log R}}, \qquad R\ge R_0. \tag{4.1}\] The constants are uniform under translation of the rectangle.

We prove this theorem through several explicit steps.

4.1. Zero overcrowding on unit segments

Lemma 4.2. For every horizontal or vertical segment \(e\) of length at most one, its BF zero count \(Z_e\) satisfies, uniformly over position, \[\PP(Z_e\ge m)\le C\exp(-c m^2\log m),\qquad m\ge m_0 . \tag{4.2}\] In particular \(\sup_e\E Z_e^2<\infty\).

Proof. On a unit line segment the restricted process has covariance \(\exp(-(u-v)^2/2)\) and spectral measure \(N(0,1)\). In the notation of [8, Theorem 1.1], \[ D_n=\max\{1,(\E|Z|^{2n})^{1/2},(\E|Z|^{2n+2})^{1/2}\}, \qquad Z\sim N(0,1), \] and the Gaussian moment formula gives \(D_n^{1/n}\le C\sqrt n\). Use that theorem with its parameter \(\epsilon=1/8\) and interval length \(T=1\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), its integer and interval-size restrictions hold, and the expression inside its logarithm is at least \(c n^{1/4}\). Its upper bound is therefore (4.2). Shorter segments are contained in unit segments. The moment assertion follows by summing the tail. ∎

4.2. A topology-preserving finite-range approximation

Realize the BF field as \[f=q*W,\qquad q(x)=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,e^{-|x|^2}, \tag{4.3}\] where \(W\) is planar white noise. Direct convolution gives \(q*q(x)=e^{-|x|^2/2}\). Choose a fixed radial smooth cutoff \(\chi\), equal to one on \(B_{1/2}\), supported in \(\overline B_1\), and set \[q_\rho(x)=q(x)\chi(x/\rho),\qquad f_\rho=q_\rho*W,\qquad \rho=K\sqrt{\log R}. \tag{4.4}\] All noise determining \(f_\rho(x)\) lies within distance \(\rho\) of \(x\).

Choose a large axis-aligned containing box \(U_R\), with integer vertices and diameter \(O(R)\), that contains \(D_R\) and a fixed positive multiple of \(R\) as a buffer. It contains every \(O(\rho)\)-box used below once \(R\) is sufficiently large. Consider all unit-grid edges contained in \(U_R\), the four boundary edges of \(D_R\) split into pieces of length at most one, and their endpoints.

Lemma 4.3 (Coupling). For every prescribed \(A>0\), \(K\) in (4.4) can be chosen so that, for all large \(R\), an event \(\mathcal T_R\) of probability at least \(1-CR^{-A}\) has the following properties:

  1. \(N_R(f)=N_R(f_\rho)\);
  2. the two fields have the same zero count on each relevant unit-grid edge;
  3. their positive and negative crossing events in \(D_R\) agree.

Proof. Fix \(B>A+6\), put \(\eps=R^{-B}\), and write \(h=f_\rho-f\). For each fixed derivative order, \[ \|\partial^\alpha(q-q_\rho)\|_{L^2(\R^2)}^2 \le C_\alpha\rho^{C_\alpha}e^{-c\rho^2}. \] Local Sobolev embedding on a unit box, using derivatives through order four on a slightly larger box, gives \[ \E\|h\|_{C^1(\text{unit box})}^2 \le C\rho^{C}e^{-c\rho^2}. \] Markov's inequality and a cover by \(O(R^2)\) unit boxes imply \[\PP(\|h\|_{C^1(U_R)}>\eps) \le CR^2\eps^{-2}\rho^C e^{-c\rho^2} \le CR^{-A} \tag{4.5}\] when \(K\) is sufficiently large. The same Sobolev argument and Gaussian moments show, for any fixed \(p\ge2\), \[ \E\|f\|_{C^2(\text{unit box})}^p\le C_p. \] Consequently, on taking \(p>A+4\), \[\PP(\|f\|_{C^2(U_R)}>R)\le C_pR^{2-p}\le CR^{-A}. \tag{4.6}\] Enlarge \(U_R\) slightly in these displays if needed to apply Sobolev embedding at its boundary.

On the complement of the event in (4.6), \((f,\nabla f)\) is \(CR\)-Lipschitz. If \(|f|+|\nabla f|\le10\eps\) somewhere, an \(\eps/(CR)\)-mesh has a point where these three Gaussian coordinates are \(O(\eps)\). There are \(O(R^4\eps^{-2})\) mesh points and their joint densities are uniformly bounded. Therefore the probability of such a near-critical point, together with the \(C^2\) bound, is at most \[CR^4\eps. \tag{4.7}\] On the relevant edges use instead \((f,\partial_\tau f)\). Their total length is \(O(R^2)\), and the number of edges is \(O(R^2)\), so the corresponding mesh has \(O(R^3\eps^{-1})\) points. The failure probability is at most \(CR^3\eps\). At all endpoints the bound is \(CR^2\eps\). The densities are uniformly bounded because these BF Gaussian vectors have fixed nondegenerate covariance; only horizontal and vertical directions occur.

On the complement of these events and (4.5), the homotopy \[ f_u=f+u h,\qquad 0\le u\le1, \] has no zero-valued critical point in \(D_R\), no zero-valued tangential critical point on its boundary or on any relevant grid edge, and no zero at any of the endpoints or corners. Apply the box-isotopy lemma [4, Lemma 12] to \(D_R\): its nodal set is carried to that of \(f_u\) by an isotopy preserving each boundary stratum, normalized to start at the identity. This preserves the pairing of boundary endpoints, hence \(N_R\). It also preserves signs on the complementary regions: along the isotopy, the value at the image of a point outside the initial zero set is continuous and never zero. Separately, on each grid edge, simple-root continuation and nonvanishing at the endpoints preserve the number of zeros. Thus no single global isotopy of the whole grid arrangement is required. The displayed failure bounds, with \(B>A+6\), prove the lemma. ∎

We will also use strict-positive rectangular RSW: for each fixed-aspect rectangle, the probability of a transverse strictly positive BF crossing is bounded below by a positive constant uniformly in scale [3, Proposition 4.11(1)]. A transverse strict-positive crossing excludes a nodal crossing between the marked sides. Hence Lemma 4.3 implies that for some \(c_0>0\) and all large \(R\), \[\PP(f_\rho\text{ has a marked-side nodal crossing in }D_R) \le1-c_0 . \tag{4.8}\] Using a strict-positive blocker is important: a nonnegative path could intersect a nodal path at a zero.

4.3. Planar separation from local congestion

Choose \(A>6\) henceforth. By Lemma 4.2 and a union bound over the \(O(R^2)\) relevant unit-grid edges, with \[m_R=C_A\sqrt{\frac{\log R}{\log\log R}}, \tag{4.9}\] the probability that some such edge has more than \(m_R\) zeros of \(f\) is at most \(CR^{-A}\). Let \(\mathcal G_R\) be the intersection of this event's complement with \(\mathcal T_R\). Then \[\PP(\mathcal G_R^c)\le CR^{-A}, \tag{4.10}\] and every relevant unit-grid edge also has at most \(m_R\) zeros of \(f_\rho\). Thus every integer-grid box of side lengths at most \(C_1(\rho+1)\) has at most \[C_2(\rho+1)m_R \tag{4.11}\] zeros on its boundary. The constants \(C_1,C_2\) below are fixed geometric constants; the buffer in \(U_R\) allows these boxes to protrude beyond \(D_R\).

Lemma 4.4 (Ordered-crosscut separation). On \(\mathcal G_R\), from any \(k\) disjoint proper nodal crosscuts of \(f_\rho\) in \(D_R\) one can select at least \(\lfloor k/M_R\rfloor\) whose mutual Euclidean distances exceed \(2\rho+2\sqrt2\), where \[M_R=\left\lceil C_3(\rho+1)m_R\right\rceil \le C\frac{\log R}{\sqrt{\log\log R}}. \tag{4.12}\]

Proof. Order the crosscuts from bottom to top as \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_k\). Every intermediate crosscut separates the rectangle into an upper and a lower part. Therefore any path in the rectangle joining a point of \(\gamma_i\) to a point of \(\gamma_j\), \(i<j\), intersects each \(\gamma_\ell\), \(i<\ell<j\). This also holds when the points are on the marked boundary sides.

Suppose points on \(\gamma_i,\gamma_j\) have distance at most \(d=2\rho+2\sqrt2+2\). The straight chord joining them stays inside the convex rectangle. Put that chord strictly inside an integer-grid box of side lengths at most \(C_1(\rho+1)\). Every intermediate crosscut meets this box, and must leave it because its horizontal extent is \(aR\), whereas the box diameter is \(O(\rho)=o(R)\). The disjoint crosscuts therefore give distinct zeros on the boundary of this box. By (4.11), \(j-i-1\le C_2(\rho+1)m_R\). Choose \(C_3\) larger than this constant, with enough slack for the additive terms. Taking every \(M_R\)-th crosscut gives the assertion. The argument uses actual nodal crosscuts, not curves in a thickened nodal set. ∎

4.4. BKR with whole white-noise boxes as coordinates

We give the underlying product-space construction explicitly. Partition the plane into half-open unit squares \(C_z=z+[0,1)^2\). The independent restrictions \(W_z=W\restriction C_z\) can be realized as random elements of \[ S_z=\{u\in H^{-2}(\R^2):\supp u\subseteq\overline C_z\}. \] Here restriction means \(W_z(\varphi)=W(\ind_{C_z}\varphi)\), using the white-noise isometry on \(L^2(\R^2)\); independence follows from orthogonality of functions supported in disjoint half-open cells. These are closed separable Hilbert subspaces, hence standard Borel spaces. Indeed the expected squared \(H^{-2}\)-norm of a white-noise restriction is finite, since \(\int_{\R^2}(1+|\xi|^2)^{-2}\dd\xi<\infty\). Convolution with \(q_\rho\in C_c^\infty\) is defined for every element of each \(S_z\), not merely for almost every white-noise sample. Only finitely many coordinates, those whose closed squares meet \(D_R+\overline B_\rho\), affect \(f_\rho\) in \(D_R\).

For a Borel event \(A\) in this finite product, a coordinate set \(J\) certifies \(A\) at \(\omega\) if every configuration agreeing with \(\omega\) on \(J\) belongs to \(A\). Let \(A^{\square n}\) denote simultaneous occurrence with \(n\) pairwise disjoint certifying coordinate sets. The product-space BKR inequality [1, Theorem 6], after coordinatewise Borel coding of the \(S_z\)'s into Borel subsets of \(\R\), states \[\PP(A^{\square n})\le\PP(A)^n . \tag{4.13}\] This formulation allows arbitrary Borel events, not only increasing ones. The completed product measure handles the measurability of the disjoint-occurrence event.

Let \(\mathcal A_\rho\) be the event that the zero set of \(f_\rho\) in \(D_R\) contains a nonempty compact connected subset meeting both marked sides. This event is Borel. To see this, the nonempty compact connected subsets of \(D_R\) meeting both closed sides form a compact family in the Hausdorff hyperspace. The condition \(K\subseteq\{g=0\}\) is closed in \(C(D_R)\) times this family, and projection over a compact space preserves closedness. The map from the finitely many \(S_z\)-coordinates to \(f_\rho|_{D_R}\in C(D_R)\) is continuous. For regular fields the event is exactly the usual nodal-path crossing event. Defining it on all continuous fields is useful for the arbitrary coordinate completions in the next argument.

Lemma 4.5 (Separated crossings have disjoint certificates). If \(f_\rho\) has \(n\) proper nodal crosscuts whose mutual distances exceed \(2\rho+2\sqrt2\), then its noise configuration belongs to \(\mathcal A_\rho^{\square n}\).

Proof. For a particular crosscut \(\gamma\), fix every coordinate \(W_z\) whose closed square meets \(\gamma+\overline B_\rho\). This fixes \(f_\rho(x)\) for every \(x\in\gamma\), because the convolution kernel has support in \(\overline B_\rho\). Thus every completion of the other coordinates retains \(\gamma\subseteq\{f_\rho=0\}\) and belongs to \(\mathcal A_\rho\), even if the completed field is nongeneric. If two curves used the same coordinate, points on them would have distance at most \(2\rho+\sqrt2\). The assumed separation therefore makes their certifying coordinate sets disjoint. There are finitely many relevant box coordinates; no finiteness of the number of scalar Gaussian coefficients inside a box is asserted or needed. ∎

Proof of Theorem 4.1. On \(\mathcal G_R\), \(N_R(f)=N_R(f_\rho)\). Lemmas 4.4 and 4.5, followed by (4.13) and (4.8), give, for \(k\ge1\), \[\PP(N_R(f)\ge k,\mathcal G_R) \le (1-c_0)^{\lfloor k/M_R\rfloor}. \tag{4.14}\] This is an unconditional BKR bound: we never condition the product measure on \(\mathcal G_R\). The good event supplies only a deterministic implication into the disjoint-occurrence event. Summing (4.14) over \(k\) gives \(\E[N_R(f)\ind_{\mathcal G_R}]\le C M_R\).

For the exceptional event, \(N_R(f)\) is bounded by the number of zeros on the left side of \(D_R\). Partition that side into \(O(R)\) segments of length at most one. Lemma 4.2 and Minkowski's inequality, without any independence assumption, give \(\|N_R(f)\|_{L^2}\le CR\). Hence \[ \E[N_R(f)\ind_{\mathcal G_R^c}] \le CR\,\PP(\mathcal G_R^c)^{1/2} \le CR^{1-A/2}. \] Together with (4.12) this proves (4.1). Regularity and transversality of \(f_\rho\) hold almost surely: its spectral density is \(|\widehat q_\rho|^2\), whose support contains an open set, so the required jets are nondegenerate. Alternatively all path-geometry arguments above are restricted to \(\mathcal G_R\), where the requisite topology and transversality were explicitly preserved. Stationarity makes the estimates uniform under translation. ∎

4.5. From rectangular crosscuts to annular through-arcs

Corollary 4.6. For fixed \(0<\alpha<\beta<\infty\), there are \(C,R_0>0\) such that \[\E N_0(\alpha R,\beta R) \le C\frac{\log R}{\sqrt{\log\log R}},\qquad R\ge R_0 . \tag{4.15}\] No uniformity as \(\beta-\alpha\downarrow0\) is asserted.

Proof. Let \(h=(\beta-\alpha)R/10\), and cover \(\partial B_{\alpha R}\) by the squares of an axis-aligned \(h\)-grid meeting it. Their number depends only on \(\alpha,\beta\). Every radial through-arc starts in one of these squares \(S\) and must leave the concentric square \(3S\), since the distance between its endpoints is at least \((\beta-\alpha)R\). At its first exit, suppose it leaves through the top side of \(3S\). Take its last earlier visit to the horizontal line through the top side of \(S\). The intervening segment is a vertical crosscut of the fixed top strip of \(3S\setminus S\), a rectangle of width \(3h\) and height \(h\). The other exit directions give three analogous strips. For almost every realization these deterministic strip boundaries are transverse and their vertices are nonzero, so no corner ambiguity occurs.

Assign each annular through-arc to one such strip. Disjoint annular arcs give disjoint crosscuts within each strip, and there are only a shape-dependent finite number of strips. Bound the annular count by the sum of their rectangular counts and apply Theorem 4.1, with the marked sides chosen for the crossing direction. ∎

5. Closing the flow estimate

Proof of the upper bound in Theorem 1.1. Fix \(0<\delta<1/\sqrt8\), put \(r=\delta\sqrt t\), and set \(\eps_0=16\delta^2<2\). The initial annulus in Proposition 3.3 has radii \[ \alpha_\delta\sqrt t,\quad \beta_\delta\sqrt t,\qquad \alpha_\delta=\sqrt{2+\delta^2},\quad \beta_\delta=\sqrt{2+4\delta^2}. \] These constants are distinct and fixed; \(\delta\) does not vary with time. Corollary 4.6 and Lemma 2.3 imply, for almost every sufficiently large \(t\), \[a(t)+\eps_0 t a'(t)\le C_\delta t^{-1/2}H(t), \qquad H(t)=\frac{\log t}{\sqrt{\log\log t}} . \tag{5.1}\] The replacement of \(H(\sqrt t)\) by \(H(t)\) changes only constants. The function \(H\) is increasing for all sufficiently large \(t\). Let \(p=1/\eps_0>1/2\), and choose \(t_0\) beyond all thresholds. Multiplying (5.1) by \(p t^{p-1}\) and using absolute continuity, \[ t^p a(t)\le t_0^p a(t_0) +C_\delta p\int_{t_0}^t s^{p-3/2}H(s)\dd s \le t_0^p a(t_0)+ \frac{C_\delta p}{p-\tfrac12}H(t)t^{p-1/2}. \] Divide by \(t^p\) and absorb the faster-decaying homogeneous term. This proves the asserted upper bound. In particular the direction of the differential inequality gives an upper bound on length density. ∎

Proof of the lower bound in Theorem 1.1. The nodal-circuit version of BF RSW [3, Remark 4.10] provides \(p_*>0\) and a fixed \(K>2\) such that, uniformly for all large \(R\), with probability at least \(p_*\) there is an initial nodal loop surrounding \(B_{2R}\) and contained in \(B_{KR}\). Regularity of the nodal set means that the circuit is an entire nodal component. Its filled area is at least \(4\pi R^2\). At time \(R^2\), (1.4) leaves area at least \(2\pi R^2\), while convex-hull containment keeps the descendant in \(B_{KR}\). The isoperimetric inequality gives its length at least \(2\sqrt2\pi R\). Consequently \[ a(R^2)\pi K^2R^2=\E\mu_{R^2}(B_{KR}) \ge p_*\,2\sqrt2\pi R . \] This gives \(a(t)\ge c/\sqrt t\) for large \(t\). The two bounds on \(L(t)=1/a(t)\) follow by taking reciprocals. ∎

6. Scope and the unresolved sharp estimate

The proof is specific to planar embedded curve shortening. It makes no claim for higher-dimensional mean curvature flow. The use of compact nodal components concerns the zero level of the centered BF field.

The two losses in (4.1) are visible: \(\rho\asymp\sqrt{\log R}\) is the truncation range needed for a polynomially small global topology error, and \(m_R\asymp\sqrt{\log R/\log\log R}\) controls the worst unit-edge zero count across the box. Their product bounds the number of strands that must be discarded to obtain disjoint noise certificates. No optimality of this logarithm is claimed.

The same deterministic reduction would prove \(a(t)\le C t^{-1/2}\) if one had \[ \sup_{R\ge R_0}\E N_0(\alpha R,\beta R)<\infty \] for each fixed annulus shape used in the argument. That uniform multiplicity estimate is not proved in this note. Neither a numerical arm exponent nor convergence to a percolation scaling limit is used to obtain the weaker bound (1.3).
